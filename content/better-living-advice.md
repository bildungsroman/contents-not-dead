Most advice fails not because it is wrong, but because it is too big. "Change
your life" is not an instruction; it is a mood. So here is smaller advice, the
kind you can actually act on before lunch.

## Do the two-minute version first

If a task feels heavy, do the two-minute version of it. Not the whole run — the
shoes. Not the essay — the first ugly sentence. Momentum is a chemical, and you
manufacture it by starting.

## Make the good choice the easy choice

Willpower is overrated and, worse, unreliable. Instead of resolving to snack
less, put the fruit where your hand already goes. Instead of resolving to read
more, leave the book on the pillow. Design beats discipline.

## Keep a "done" list

Everyone keeps a to-do list, which is really a monument to everything you have
failed to finish. Try keeping a "done" list instead. At the end of the day,
write what you actually did. It is quietly astonishing.

## Let some things be mediocre on purpose

You do not have to optimize your cereal. Reserve your excellence for the two or
three things that matter and let the rest be gloriously average. This is not
laziness; it is triage.

> The goal is not a perfect day. The goal is a day you would be willing to
> repeat.

That is the whole advice. Do the small version, make good choices cheap, notice
what you finished, and refuse to be excellent at everything. Tomorrow, do it
again.
