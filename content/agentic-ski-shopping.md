Ask an agent to "buy me a good all-mountain ski" and you'll get a good all-mountain ski. Whether it's the right one depends on details that rarely make it into a product listing: how much rocker it has, what's inside the core, and whether it wants to carve or slash. Here are three of the most popular skis in the 98–102 mm class, and the context your agent needs to choose between them.

## Blizzard Rustler 10: the quiver-of-one

The [Rustler 10](https://www.blizzard-tecnica.com/us/en/collection/men/skis/rustler/rustler-10-flat-2) is Blizzard's middle child in a freeride family that also includes the narrower Rustler 9 and the wider Rustler 11. At 134/102/123 mm with a 17.5 m radius (180 cm), it's built for skiers who split their days between groomers, trees, and whatever the wind left behind.

Blizzard redesigned the Rustler in 2024. Its FluxForm construction wraps Titanal around the edges rather than running a sheet through the middle, over a Trueblend Free core of beech, poplar, and paulownia. The result grips well on hardpack without getting heavy, at roughly 2 kg per ski at 180 cm. The rocker/camber/rocker profile has a lot of tip splay, so it floats better than its waist suggests. Reviewers note it's a bit more serious and less flickable than the original Rustler, which is a fair trade for how stable it is. Lengths run from 162 to 192 cm, and the women's version is the Sheeva 10.

## Atomic Bent 100: the playful one

The [Bent 100](https://www.atomic.com/en-us/products/bent-100-aa7366-202600) is the ski for people who treat the whole mountain as a terrain park. It carries Chris Benchetler's artwork and a 128.5/100/119 mm shape with a 19 m radius (179 cm). At about 1,700 g per ski, it's noticeably lighter than the other two.

Its signature is HRZN 3D, a beveled insert wrapped around the tip and tail that adds float without adding width. Under that is a light poplar core, Dura Cap sidewalls, and a thicker Resist Edge for sliding rails. The 20/65/15 rocker profile is more directional than it looks: there's substantial camber underfoot and only modest tail rocker. That gives it real edge grip on groomers, though testers find it less composed than heavier skis when the snow turns firm and icy. It comes in 158 to 186 cm.

## Salomon QST 98: the smooth operator

Now in its third generation, the [QST 98](https://www.salomon.com/en-gb/product/qst-98-li4910) measures 133/98/121 mm with a 17 m radius (183 cm). It's the narrowest of the three, but generous rocker and taper make it float like something wider.

Salomon's trick is damping without metal. A full poplar core is reinforced with C/FX, a carbon and flax weave, and cork inserts at the ends soak up chatter in chop and crud. Double Sidewall Technology adds an extra strip of ABS underfoot, letting Salomon add rocker without giving up edge hold. It's about as heavy as the Rustler, near 2 kg at 183 cm, and it feels that way in a good sense: calm, predictable, and hard to knock off line. It comes in 169 to 189 cm.

## What to tell your agent

All three are excellent, so the best choice comes down to how you ski:

- **Mostly groomers and mixed conditions, occasional powder:** Rustler 10
- **Park laps, side hits, and skiing switch:** Bent 100
- **Charging through chop and wanting the smoothest ride:** QST 98

Give your agent your height, your ability, and where you usually ski, and let it handle sizing and checkout. The difference between a good ski and the right ski is all in the details, and those are exactly what a well-briefed agent can read for you.
